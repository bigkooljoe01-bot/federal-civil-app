52. What immediate action is required if keys to any strong-room, safe, cash tank, or box are
lost?
A. Wait for them to reappear.
B. Report immediately to the Accounting Officer, inform other key holders, seal the safe, and
place a police guard if cash is inside.
C. Replace the safe quietly.
D. Only report if a large sum of money is involved
Correct Answer: B.
Explanation: Financial Regulation 1111 outlines a detailed procedure including immediate
reporting, informing other key holders, sealing the safe, and placing a police guard if cash is
present.
53. Who is liable to meet the cost of altering the lock and providing new keys if a key to a
Government safe is lost due to negligence?
A. The Government.
B. The officer responsible for the loss.
C. The Accountant-General.
D. The supplier of the safe.
Correct Answer: B.
Explanation: Financial Regulation 1112 states, "The officer responsible for the loss of a key to a
Government safe, is liable to be called upon to meet the cost of altering the lock and the
provision of new keys, if found negligent.”
54. If a key holder suspects interference with the keys or locks of a strong-room or safe, what
must they do?
A. Ignore it unless a loss occurs.
B. Remove the contents to another safe, report the circumstances, and request immediate steps
to alter the locks and provide new keys.
C. Conduct their own investigation.
D. Inform only their immediate supervisor.
Correct Answer: B.
Explanation: Financial Regulation 1113 directs the key holder to "remove the contents to
another safe, report the circumstances as directed in Regulation 1111, and ask that immediate
steps be taken to have the locks altered and new keys provided."
55. Question: Who must approve alterations to locks of safes and the provision of new keys,
and under what conditions?
A. The Head of Department, under normal conditions.
B. The Accounting Officer, under security conditions.
C. The cashier, at their discretion.
D. The Minister of Finance, without any specific conditions.
